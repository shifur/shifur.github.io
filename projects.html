Time series forecasting · MSc dissertation · 2024

Fixed Income Trading and Macroeconomic Data: The Case of UK Inflation Forecasting

MSc in Data Analytics, School of Mathematical Sciences, Queen Mary University of London · Supervisor: Dr. Matthew Lewis

Investigated whether statistical and macroeconomic forecasting models can predict UK inflation consistently across changing economic conditions. The study used monthly Retail Prices Index (RPI) inflation, unemployment and interest rate data from January 1988 to May 2024.

Research approach: Implemented ARIMA, seasonal ARIMA (SARIMA), vector autoregression (VAR), dynamic regression with ARIMA errors, and Phillips Curve models in R. Compared 12-month held-out forecasts using RMSE, MAE, and actual-versus-forecast curves across pre-crisis, post-crisis, and full-sample periods.

Key findings: Forecasting performance varied with economic conditions. ARIMA(3,1,3) performed best among tested models in the pre-crisis period (RMSE 0.13, MAE 0.10); the three-variable VAR performed best in the post-crisis period (RMSE 0.39, MAE 0.33). The Phillips Curve achieved the lowest full-period errors in the reported comparison (RMSE 2.85, MAE 2.05), with seasonal ARIMA also performing comparatively well.

Research insight: No single model consistently outperformed all others across economic periods. The dissertation proposed exploring regime-aware hybrid forecasting and machine-learning-based model selection as future research.
